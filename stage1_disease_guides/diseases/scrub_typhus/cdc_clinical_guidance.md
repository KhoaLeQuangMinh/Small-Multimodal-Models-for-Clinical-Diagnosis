# CDC Clinical Overview: Scrub Typhus

## Pathogen and Transmission
- **Etiologic Agent:** *Orientia tsutsugamushi* (formerly *Rickettsia tsutsugamushi*).
- **Vector:** Larval trombiculid mites, known colloquially as **chiggers** (*Leptotrombidium* species).
- **Transmission Cycle:** Mites act as both the vector and the natural reservoir through transovarial and transstadial transmission. Humans become accidental dead-end hosts when bitten in overgrown scrub vegetation, agricultural fields, secondary forest regrowth, or riverbanks.

## Endemic Geography ("Tsutsugamushi Triangle")
- Traditionally recognized across an expansive geographical triangle:
  - Eastern/Southeastern Asia: Japan, Korea, China, Taiwan, Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Malaysia, Indonesia, Myanmar, Philippines.
  - South Asia: India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal.
  - Oceania & Northern Australia.
- Emerging foci have recently been reported in parts of South America (Chile) and Africa.

## Clinical Timeline & Presentation
- **Incubation Period:** Typically 7 to 10 days (range: 6 to 21 days) following the chigger bite.
- **Onset:** Characteristically sudden and abrupt.
- **Cardinal Clinical Signs:**
  - High fever, profound chills, and severe headache.
  - Generalized myalgias and arthralgias.
  - **The Eschar:** Pathognomonic necrotic lesion resembling a cigarette burn. Begins as a painless papule that ulcerates, crusts, and forms a black necrotic center surrounded by an erythematous indurated halo. Most commonly located in warm, moist, intertriginous anatomical areas (groin, perineum, axilla, under breast folds, waistband). Present in 50% to 80% of cases.
  - Regional or generalized lymphadenopathy (prominent in lymph nodes draining the eschar site).
  - Maculopapular rash on trunk spreading centrifugally to extremities around days 4 to 6.
  - Conjunctival injection / suffusion without discharge.

## Severe Complications
- Disseminated vasculitis and perivascular lymphocytic infiltration.
- Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) / non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
- Meningoencephalitis (altered mental status, delirium, neck stiffness).
- Acute kidney injury (AKI), myocarditis, and septic shock / Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS).

## Empirical Diagnostic Clues & Treatment
- **Drug of Choice:** Doxycycline (100 mg orally or IV twice daily).
- **Therapeutic Hallmark:** Dramatic, rapid clinical improvement with complete defervescence typically within 24 to 48 hours of starting Doxycycline.
- **Alternative:** Azithromycin (especially in pregnancy or severe pediatric cases).
- Resistance/Failure: Lack of defervescence after 48 to 72 hours of appropriate Doxycycline strongly suggests an alternate diagnosis (e.g. Melioidosis, severe Dengue, or Malaria).
