# Foodborne Trematodes - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agents & Organ Distribution:** Caused by four major groups of digenetic foodborne trematodes (flukes) affecting >56 million people globally:
  - **1. Liver Flukes (Biliary Ducts):**
    - *Clonorchis sinensis* (Chinese liver fluke): Endemic in East Asia (China, South Korea, northern Vietnam, eastern Russia).
    - *Opisthorchis viverrini* (Southeast Asian liver fluke): Endemic in the lower Mekong basin (northeast Thailand, Laos, Cambodia) and *Opisthorchis felineus* (Russia, Eastern Europe).
    - *Fasciola hepatica* (common sheep liver fluke) and *Fasciola gigantica* (giant liver fluke): Cosmopolitan distribution in livestock-raising regions (Andean highlands of Bolivia and Peru, Nile delta in Egypt, northern Iran, Europe).
  - **2. Lung Flukes (Pulmonary Parenchyma):**
    - *Paragonimus westermani* (Oriental lung fluke) and related species (*P. africanus*, *P. mexicanus*): Endemic in East and Southeast Asia (China, the Philippines, Japan, Korea), parts of Africa, and Central/South America.
  - **3. Intestinal Flukes:** *Fasciolopsis buski* (giant intestinal fluke), *Heterophyes heterophyes*, *Metagonimus yokogawai*.
- **Transmission Pathways & Dietary Exposures:**
  - Mandatory complex life cycle involving a freshwater snail first intermediate host:
  - **Clonorchiasis & Opisthorchiasis:** Ingestion of raw, undercooked, pickled, fermented, or sun-dried freshwater cyprinid fish (e.g., carp) harboring encysted infectious **metacercariae** (traditional dishes: Thai *koi pla*, Chinese raw fish porridge, sashimi).
  - **Fascioliasis:** Ingestion of raw, wild, unwashed freshwater aquatic plants harboring encysted metacercariae (principally **watercress** [*Nasturtium officinale*], water spinach, water chestnuts, mint), or drinking unboiled surface water in pastoral grazing zones.
  - **Paragonimiasis:** Ingestion of raw, undercooked, salted, marinated, or wine-soaked freshwater **crustaceans (river crabs, crayfish)** containing metacercariae (e.g., traditional "drunken crabs" in China, fresh crab soup in Southeast Asia).
- **Incubation Periods:**
  - Fascioliasis: Acute hepatic migration phase begins 1 to 6 weeks post-ingestion; biliary phase begins at 3 to 4 months.
  - Clonorchiasis / Opisthorchiasis: 3 to 4 weeks to patency; chronic symptoms evolve over years.
  - Paragonimiasis: 2 to 12 weeks to pulmonary encapsulation; can persist for decades.

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
The clinical manifestation depends entirely on the anatomical organ colonized:
- **1. Clonorchiasis & Opisthorchiasis (Chronic Biliary Disease & Carcinogenesis):**
  - Adult worms (10–25 mm) dwell inside medium and small intrahepatic bile ducts for up to 20–30 years.
  - Symptoms correlate with worm burden: Dull right upper quadrant abdominal pain, anorexia, weight loss, recurrent cholangitis, acalculous cholecystitis, and multiple intrahepatic biliary strictures with recurrent pyogenic cholangiohepatitis.
  - **Pathognomonic Oncogenic Complication (Cholangiocarcinoma):** Both *C. sinensis* and *O. viverrini* are classified as Group 1 definitive human biological carcinogens by IARC/WHO. Chronic mechanical irritation, parasite secretome (granulin), and endogenous nitrosamine synthesis drive malignant transformation into **intrahepatic or perihilar Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)** in individuals with long-standing raw fish diets.
- **2. Fascioliasis (Hepatic Tunnelling & Biliary Obstruction):**
  - **Acute / Invasive Hepatic Phase (Weeks 1–12):** Juvenile flukes penetrate the duodenal wall, cross the peritoneal cavity, pierce Glisson's capsule, and burrow mechanically through the hepatic parenchyma toward the bile ducts.
    - Manifestations: High continuous or spiking fever ($39–40^\circ\text{C}$), severe right upper quadrant abdominal pain, painful hepatomegaly, urticaria, facial angioedema, and **astronomical peripheral blood eosinophilia**.
  - **Chronic / Biliary Phase (Months to Years):** Adult flukes (20–30 mm) reside in the major bile ducts and gallbladder.
    - Manifestations: Intermittent obstructive jaundice, biliary colic mimicking choledocholithiasis, cholangitis, and biliary fibrosis without malignancy.
- **3. Paragonimiasis (Pulmonary Pseudotuberculosis):**
  - Juvenile flukes penetrate the gut, cross the peritoneal cavity, bore through the diaphragm, and encapsulate in pairs within cystic cavities in the lung parenchyma connecting with the bronchioles.
  - **Cardinal Pulmonary Triad:**
    1. Chronic, indolent productive cough lasting weeks to months.
    2. **"Rusty-Brown" / "Chocolate-Sauce" Hemoptysis:** Expectoration of thick, gelatinous, brown-tinged sputum containing clotted blood, desquamated respiratory cells, Charcot-Leyden crystals, and masses of operculated parasite eggs.
    3. Pleuritic chest pain and dyspnea.
  - **Ectopic Cerebral Paragonimiasis:** Migration of worms via connective tissue around the jugular vein into the cranium; presents with adult-onset focal epilepsy, visual field defects, intracerebral hemorrhage, and chronic intracranial hypertension.

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
  - **Universal, Massive Peripheral Blood Eosinophilia:**
    - In Acute Fascioliasis: The single most striking laboratory finding; eosinophils often comprise **$> 40–80\%$ of the differential count** (absolute eosinophil count frequently $10,000–35,000/\mu\text{L}$), accompanying a marked leukocytosis ($15,000–40,000/\mu\text{L}$).
    - In Paragonimiasis: Prominent persistent eosinophilia ($> 15–35\%$) and leukocytosis.
    - In Chronic Clonorchiasis: Mild-to-moderate eosinophilia ($5–15\%$).
  - Elevated serum IgE levels (routinely $> 1,000–5,000\text{ IU/mL}$).
- **Hepatic Enzymes & Inflammatory Chemistry:**
  - Serum Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) and Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT) are markedly elevated, reflecting chronic biliary epithelial irritation.
  - Mild-to-moderate transaminitis (AST and ALT $2–4\times$ ULN); serum direct bilirubin is elevated during acute biliary obstruction.
  - Elevated acute-phase reactants (CRP and ESR) during invasive phases.
- **Diagnostic Confirmation at Triage:**
  - **Sputum Microscopy (*Paragonimus*):** Microscopic examination of unstained sputum or centrifuged 24-hour sputum shows golden-brown, thick-shelled, **operculated eggs with an opercular rim/shoulder** measuring $80–120\ \mu\text{m} \times 45–65\ \mu\text{m}$.
  - **Stool Microscopy (Formalin-Ether Concentration / Kato-Katz):**
    - *Clonorchis* / *Opisthorchis:* Very small, yellowish-brown operculated eggs measuring $25–35\ \mu\text{m} \times 12–19\ \mu\text{m}$, bearing a distinctive **prominent convex operculum with an expanded shoulder collar** and a tiny knob at the posterior abopercular pole (**"pitcher / urn-shaped" appearance**).
    - *Fasciola:* Large, golden-yellow, unembryonated operculated eggs measuring $130–150\ \mu\text{m} \times 65–90\ \mu\text{m}$ (cannot be distinguished reliably from *Fasciolopsis buski*).
- **Bedside Imaging (Ultrasound & Chest Radiography at Triage):**
  - **Abdominal Ultrasound:**
    - *Clonorchiasis:* Diffuse, uniform, non-tapering dilation of the small peripheral intrahepatic bile ducts with increased periductal echogenicity, while the common bile duct remains non-dilated.
    - *Fascioliasis:* Linear, branching, serpentine hypoechoic parenchymal tracks (**"tunnels and burrows"**) in the subcapsular liver parenchyma during acute migration; mobile, non-shadowing leaf-like structures inside the gallbladder or common bile duct during chronic disease.
  - **Chest Radiography (*Paragonimus*):** Thin- or thick-walled, round, ring-shaped cystic lesions ($1–3\text{ cm}$), patchy nodular opacities, pleural thickening, or spontaneous pneumothorax, mimicking apical or mid-zone pulmonary tuberculosis.
  - **Head CT / MRI (Cerebral Paragonimiasis):** Clustered, multilocular, calcified cystic lesions with ring enhancement demonstrating the pathognomonic **"soap-bubble appearance"**.

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Pulmonary Tuberculosis (*M. tuberculosis*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Chronic cough, hemoptysis, night sweats, cavitary infiltrates on chest radiography.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Tuberculosis features normal or low eosinophil counts, negative dietary raw crustacean history, and positive acid-fast bacilli on sputum smear or GeneXpert. Paragonimiasis exhibits **marked peripheral eosinophilia ($> 15–30\%$)**, raw crab ingestion, and operculated eggs in sputum wet mount.
- **Amoebic Liver Abscess (*E. histolytica*):**
  - *Differentiator:* High fever, right upper quadrant pain, hepatic hypoechoic mass.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Amoebic liver abscess features a solitary round liquefactive cavity with anchovy-paste pus, zero peripheral eosinophilia, and positive amoebic serology. Acute fascioliasis features branching serpentine subcapsular tracks, massive eosinophilia ($> 40\%$), and raw watercress history.
- **Acute Bacterial Cholangitis / Choledocholithiasis:**
  - *Differentiator:* Biliary colic, jaundice, fever.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Bacterial cholangitis features high neutrophilic leukocytosis without eosinophilia, and gallstones on ultrasound. Clonorchiasis features peripheral eosinophilia, raw fish diet, and peripheral ductal dilation.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Absence of any dietary exposure to raw/undercooked freshwater fish, crabs, crayfish, or aquatic plants (watercress) completely rules OUT foodborne trematode infection.
  - Total absence of peripheral blood eosinophilia ($< 3\%$) during suspected acute hepatic or pulmonary migration rules OUT acute fascioliasis or acute paragonimiasis.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **Praziquantel (Universal Drug for Clonorchis, Opisthorchis, Paragonimus):**
  - **Clonorchiasis & Opisthorchiasis:** **Praziquantel** 25 mg/kg orally three times daily (total 75 mg/kg/day) for 2 to 3 days, taken with food. Clears $>90–100\%$ of adult worms.
  - **Paragonimiasis:** **Praziquantel** 25 mg/kg orally three times daily for 3 consecutive days. Halts cough and rusty hemoptysis within 7 to 14 days. Alternative: Triclabendazole (20 mg/kg in two divided doses).
- **CRITICAL EXCEPTION: Fascioliasis is INTRINSICALLY REFRACTORY to Praziquantel:**
  - **WARNING:** Praziquantel has ZERO clinical activity against *Fasciola hepatica* and *Fasciola gigantica*. Prescribing praziquantel for fascioliasis results in total therapeutic failure.
  - **Triclabendazole (The Sole Effective Drug for Fascioliasis):**
    - A benzimidazole derivative with specific fasciolicidal activity against both juvenile migratory and adult flukes:
    - Oral **Triclabendazole** (10 mg/kg single dose, or 20 mg/kg divided into two doses 12 hours apart in heavy infections) administered with a fat-rich meal to enhance intestinal absorption. Produces rapid defervescence and $>95\%$ cure.
    - Second-line alternative: Nitazoxanide (500 mg BID for 7 days, inferior cure rate ~50–60%).
- **Antibiotic Refractoriness:** Completely unresponsive to broad-spectrum antibacterials, cephalosporins, or antimalarials.
