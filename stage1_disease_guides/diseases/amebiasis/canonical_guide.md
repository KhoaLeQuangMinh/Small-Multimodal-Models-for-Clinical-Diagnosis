# Amebiasis - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agent & Pathogenicity:** Caused by *Entamoeba histolytica*, an invasive, pathogenic, anaerobic protozoan parasite capable of tissue destruction. It must be strictly differentiated from non-pathogenic, morphologically identical commensal species (*Entamoeba dispar*, *Entamoeba moshkovskii*, and *Entamoeba bangladeshi*).
- **Life Cycle & Transmission Pathways:**
  - Exclusively fecal-oral route through ingestion of infectious, quadri-nucleate, chlorine-resistant cysts in fecally contaminated drinking water or raw unwashed vegetables/fruits (often fertilized with human nightsoil).
  - Person-to-person transmission in crowded domestic environments, institutionalized settings, day-care centers, and via oral-anal sexual contact (especially among men who have sex with men [MSM]).
  - Excystation in the terminal ileum releases motile **trophozoites** (15–40 $\mu$m) that utilize Gal/GalNAc lectin for enterocyte adherence and secrete pore-forming amoebapores and cysteine proteinases to invade the colonic epithelium.
- **Geographic Endemicity & Reservoirs:** Humans are the primary maintenance reservoir. Widespread in tropical and subtropical regions with deficient municipal water sanitation: the Indian subcontinent, sub-Saharan Africa, Mexico, Central and South America, and parts of Southeast Asia.
- **Demographic Vulnerability:**
  - Intestinal disease affects both sexes equally.
  - **Amoebic Liver Abscess (ALA)** exhibits an overwhelming male predilection, occurring **7 to 10 times more frequently in adult men aged 20 to 50 years** than in females, with heavy alcohol consumption and dietary iron serving as potent cofactors.
- **Incubation Period:** Highly variable: 2 to 4 weeks (ranges from a few days to several months or even years post-travel).

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
Manifests in two primary distinct clinical syndromes:
- **1. Amoebic Colitis / Dysentery (Intestinal Amebiasis):**
  - **Insidious Subacute Onset:** Develops gradually over 1 to 3 weeks (contrasting with the hyperacute, explosive onset of bacterial dysentery).
  - Progressive crampy lower abdominal pain, localized right- or left-lower quadrant abdominal tenderness, and frequent bowel movements (6–12 stools/day) containing dark red altered blood and mucus (**"currant jelly" or maroon bloody dysentery**).
  - **Low or Absent Fever:** High spiking fever is characteristically **absent in 60–80% of patients with uncomplicated amoebic colitis** (a crucial distinction from shigellosis or salmonellosis).
  - **Pathology & Colonoscopy:** Classic localized, discrete **"flask-shaped" mucosal ulcers** with collar-like margins and normal intervening mucosa; trophozoites display pathognomonic **erythrophagocytosis** (ingestion of host erythrocytes).
  - **Severe Intestinal Complications:**
    - **Fulminant Necrotizing Colitis:** Toxic presentation with high fever, severe abdominal distension, rebound tenderness, peritonitis, colonic gangrene, and bowel perforation (mortality $> 40\%$).
    - **Amoeboma:** A localized, chronic, inflammatory, exophytic, hyperplastic pseudotumor/granulomatous mass of the colon (most common in the cecum or ascending colon) that presents with right lower quadrant pain and an palpable mass mimicking cecal adenocarcinoma.
- **2. Amoebic Liver Abscess (ALA - Extraintestinal Amebiasis):**
  - Most common extraintestinal manifestation, resulting from hematogenous dissemination of trophozoites through the portal venous system.
  - **Paradoxical History:** Over 70–80% of patients with amoebic liver abscess have **no concurrent diarrhea or gastrointestinal symptoms** at presentation.
  - **Acute Febrile Course:** Presents within 1 to 2 weeks of symptom onset with high continuous or remittent fever ($38.5–40^\circ\text{C}$), shaking chills, drenching diaphoresis, and dull, aching right upper quadrant (RUQ) abdominal pain radiating to the right shoulder or epigastrium.
  - **Physical Examination:** Point tenderness over the liver on intercostal percussion or palpation (positive liver Murphy sign); smooth, tender hepatomegaly; and dullness at the right lung base.
  - **Pathognomonic "Anchovy Sauce" Pus:** Needle aspiration of the abscess cavity yields a thick, homogeneous, odorless, reddish-brown or chocolate-colored necrotic fluid with the classic appearance of **"anchovy paste" or "chocolate pudding"**, consisting of sterile liquefactive necrosis of hepatocytes (trophozoites are found only in the living outer fibrous wall, not in the necrotic center).
  - **Rupture Complications:**
    - Superior rupture through the diaphragm into the pleural cavity (amoebic empyema) or bronchial tree, causing a **hepatobronchial fistula** where the patient coughs up cups of anchovy-paste sputum.
    - Rupture of left lobe abscesses into the pericardial sac causing acute fatal cardiac tamponade.

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
  - **Marked Neutrophilic Leukocytosis:** Total WBC $12,000–25,000/\mu\text{L}$ with left shift ($> 10\%$ band forms) in $>80\%$ of amoebic liver abscess cases.
  - Mild-to-moderate normocytic normochromic anemia.
  - **Complete Absence of Eosinophilia:** Unlike helminthic infections, protozoan amoebiasis does NOT stimulate peripheral blood eosinophilia.
- **Hepatic & Inflammatory Markers:**
  - Serum Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) is elevated ($2–4\times$ ULN) in $>75–80\%$ of liver abscess cases.
  - Mild-to-moderate transaminitis (AST and ALT $1.5–3\times$ ULN); serum total bilirubin is usually normal or only mildly elevated ($< 2–3\text{ mg/dL}$). Marked jaundice occurs in $< 10\%$ and indicates massive parenchymal compression or biliary fistula.
  - High inflammatory response: CRP is markedly elevated ($> 100–200\text{ mg/L}$); ESR $> 60–100\text{ mm/h}$.
- **Diagnostic Confirmation at Triage:**
  - **Stool Diagnostics:** Microscopy of warm fresh stool demonstrates motile trophozoites containing ingested red blood cells (**erythrophagocytosis** is diagnostic of *E. histolytica* vs. *E. dispar*). Fecal antigen detection by ELISA (targeting Gal/GalNAc lectin) or stool PCR distinguishes pathogenic *E. histolytica*.
  - **Serum Amoebic Serology:** Indirect hemagglutination (IHA) or IgG ELISA has $>95\%$ sensitivity in amoebic liver abscess after day 7 of symptoms.
- **Bedside Imaging (Ultrasound & CT at Triage):**
  - **Abdominal Ultrasound:** Demonstrates a **solitary (in 70–85% of cases), round or oval, well-defined hypoechoic lesion** with low-level internal acoustic echoes, located predominantly in the **posterior superior segment of the right hepatic lobe (segment VII or VIII)**, immediately abutting the liver capsule. Absence of a thick calcified rim or internal daughter vesicles.
  - **Chest Radiography:** Elevation of the right hemidiaphragm, right lower lobe atelectasis, and sympathetic right pleural effusion.

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Pyogenic Liver Abscess (*E. coli*, *K. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Right upper quadrant pain, fever, hepatic mass.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Pyogenic abscess occurs equally in older males/females with underlying biliary stones, diabetes, or malignancy; lesions are frequently multiple and bilobar; aspirated pus is foul-smelling, green/yellow, and loaded with bacteria; requires prolonged IV antibiotic coverage and drainage. Amoebic abscess occurs predominantly in young/middle-aged males, is solitary, produces odorless "anchovy paste" pus, is bacteria-free, and cures rapidly with metronidazole.
- **Echinococcosis (Hydatid Cyst - *Echinococcus granulosus*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Cystic mass in the liver.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Hydatid cysts are chronic, slow-growing, afebrile, well-demarcated cystic lesions with a thick, calcified laminated wall, internal daughter cysts, and "water-lily" sign on ultrasound, accompanied by peripheral eosinophilia. Amebiasis features acute high fever, liquefactive necrotic cavity without calcified wall, and zero eosinophilia.
- **Bacillary Dysentery (*Shigella*, *Campylobacter*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Bloody diarrheal stools.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Bacillary dysentery presents hyperacutely with high spiking fever ($> 39^\circ\text{C}$), severe systemic toxicity, tenesmus, and fecal microscopy packed with sheets of neutrophils/pus cells. Amoebic colitis has subacute onset, absent/low fever, and microscopic absence of dense fecal neutrophils.
- **Colorectal Carcinoma (vs. Cecal Amoeboma):**
  - *Differentiator:* Palpable right iliac fossa mass, weight loss, altered bowel habits.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Amoeboma completely resolves on follow-up imaging within 4–6 weeks of nitroimidazole therapy; biopsy reveals granulomatous amoebic infiltration without cellular dysplasia.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Presence of significant peripheral eosinophilia ($> 1,000/\mu\text{L}$ or $> 10\%$) rules OUT amoebic liver abscess and indicates a helminthic parasite (*Fasciola hepatica*, *Toxocara*, *Echinococcus*).
  - Putrid, foul-smelling odor of aspirated abscess fluid rules OUT uncomplicated amoebic liver abscess.
  - Negative serum amoebic serology ($> 7$ days after onset) makes amoebic liver abscess highly unlikely.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **The Mandatory Two-Step Therapeutic Protocol:**
  - **Step 1: Tissue Amebicide (Eradicates invasive trophozoites in liver and colonic wall):**
    - Oral or intravenous **Metronidazole** (750 mg orally or IV three times daily for 7 to 10 days) OR oral **Tinidazole** (2 g once daily for 3 to 5 days).
    - **Dramatic Clinical Response:** Defervescence, resolution of night sweats, and marked improvement of right upper quadrant pain occurs within **48 to 72 hours** of starting metronidazole. Failure to improve by 72 hours mandates consideration of pyogenic abscess, diagnostic needle aspiration, or secondary bacterial superinfection.
  - **Step 2: Luminal Amebicide (Mandatory Eradication of Intestinal Cysts):**
    - Even if the patient has a solitary liver abscess and no diarrhea, a luminal agent MUST follow tissue amebicides to eliminate persistent intestinal cysts and prevent late relapse or onward transmission:
    - Oral **Paromomycin** (25–35 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses for 7 days) OR oral **Diloxanide furoate** (500 mg three times daily for 10 days) OR oral **Iodoquinol** (650 mg three times daily for 20 days).
- **Indications for Needle Aspiration / Percutaneous Drainage:**
  - Routine drainage is NOT necessary for most amoebic liver abscesses. Aspiration is reserved for:
    1. Large abscesses ($> 5–10\text{ cm}$) with impending risk of rupture.
    2. Left hepatic lobe abscesses (high hazard of rupture into the pericardial sac).
    3. Failure of clinical defervescence after 72 hours of metronidazole.
    4. Diagnostic uncertainty (suspicion of pyogenic abscess).
- **Antibiotic Refractoriness:** Ineffective against standard antibacterial penicillins, aminoglycosides, or macrolides without nitroimidazoles.
