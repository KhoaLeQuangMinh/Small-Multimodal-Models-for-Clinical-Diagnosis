# Neurocysticercosis - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agent & Life Cycle Duality:** Caused by the larval metacestode stage (**cysticerci**) of the cyclophyllidean pork tapeworm, *Taenia solium*, colonizing the central nervous system (CNS).
  - **Critical Etiologic Distinction (Ingestion of Eggs vs. Pork):**
    - Intestinal Taeniasis (harboring an adult tapeworm in the gut) occurs from eating undercooked pork containing viable larval cysticerci.
    - **Neurocysticercosis (NCC)** is acquired exclusively through the **ingestion of microscopic *Taenia solium* eggs excreted in the feces of an infected human tapeworm carrier**.
    - Transmission occurs via the fecal-oral route through contaminated drinking water, unwashed agricultural produce, food prepared by an infected carrier, or fecal-oral autoinfection (reverse peristalsis or poor hand hygiene).
    - **CONSUMING PORK DOES NOT DIRECTLY CAUSE NEUROCYSTICERCOSIS.**
- **Global Burden & Endemicity:** The single most common identifiable cause of acquired, adult-onset epilepsy in low- and middle-income countries. Highly endemic across Latin America (Mexico, Peru, Ecuador, Colombia, Brazil), South and Southeast Asia (India, Nepal, Vietnam), and sub-Saharan Africa. Approximately 30% of all epilepsy cases in endemic rural areas are directly attributable to neurocysticercosis.
- **Latency & Clinical Incubation:** Cysticerci establish active immune evasion within the CNS and can remain viable, intact, and clinically silent for **2 to 5 years (and up to 20–30 years)** before spontaneous or anthelmintic-induced larval degeneration triggers an acute host inflammatory response.

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
The clinical presentation is governed by the number, anatomical location (parenchymal vs. extraparenchymal), and evolutionary stage of the cysticerci:
- **1. Parenchymal Neurocysticercosis (60–80% of Cases):**
  - Pathological evolution proceeds through four standardized stages:
    1. *Vesicular Stage:* Viable, thin-walled, translucent cyst containing clear fluid and an intact invaginated scolex. Asymptomatic due to active immunosuppression.
    2. *Colloidal Vesicular Stage:* Degenerating larva provokes intense host cell-mediated immune reaction; cyst fluid becomes turbid, capsule thickens, accompanied by dense perilesional vasogenic edema.
    3. *Granular Nodular Stage:* Cyst collapses into a solid fibrotic nodule surrounded by gliosis.
    4. *Calcified Nodular Stage:* Non-viable, shrunken, densely calcified, mineralized scar without active edema.
  - **Cardinal Presenting Hallmarks:**
    - **Adult-Onset Seizures / Epilepsy (Present in 70–90% of symptomatic parenchymal NCC):** Focal seizures (with or without secondary generalization) or generalized tonic-clonic convulsions represent the primary presenting manifestation. Seizures can be provoked by active inflammation (colloidal phase) or arise years later from chronic epileptogenic gliotic calcified scars.
    - **Chronic Headaches (35–50%):** Tension-type, throbbing, or migraine-like headaches.
    - **Focal Neurological Deficits (15–20%):** Motor weakness, hemiparesis, sensory impairment, or dysphasia corresponding to lesion cortical topography.
- **2. Extraparenchymal Neurocysticercosis (Subarachnoid / Intraventricular - Severe Forms):**
  - **Intraventricular Neurocysticercosis (10–20%):**
    - Cysts lodge within the ventricular cavities (most commonly the fourth ventricle, followed by the lateral and third ventricles).
    - **Bruns Syndrome (Pathognomonic Hallmark):** Paroxysmal, catastrophic attacks of severe occipital headache, projectile vomiting, vertiginous ataxia, and episodic syncope / sudden loss of consciousness **precipitated by abrupt rotational head movements**, caused by a mobile 4th ventricular cyst temporarily occluding CSF outflow at the aqueduct of Sylvius (**ball-valve effect**), triggering acute obstructive hydrocephalus.
  - **Subarachnoid / Racemose Neurocysticercosis (Basal Cisterns):**
    - Cysts in the Sylvian fissure or basilar cisterns expand without forming a scolex, proliferating by continuous external budding into giant, multilocular, lobulated cystic clusters resembling grapes (**racemose cysticercosis**).
    - **Severe Complications:** Chronic basilar arachnoiditis, progressive communicating hydrocephalus, cranial neuropathies (CN II, III, VI, VII), intracranial hypertension (papilledema, visual obscurations), and endarteritis obliterans leading to **ischemic cerebrovascular stroke**.

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF):**
  - **CBC:** Peripheral WBC count is normal. Mild peripheral blood eosinophilia ($5–10\%$) is present in $< 20–30\%$ of cases and its absence never excludes NCC.
  - **CSF Examination:** Indicated in suspected extraparenchymal disease or meningitis: lymphocytic pleocytosis (10–300 WBC/$\mu$L), elevated protein (50–300 mg/dL), normal or mildly decreased glucose, and elevated CSF pressure.
- **Serological Diagnostics (The Gold Standard):**
  - **Enzyme-Linked Immunoelectrotransfer Blot (EITB / Western Blot):** Utilizing lentil lectin-purified *Taenia solium* glycoprotein antigens (LL-GP). High diagnostic accuracy ($>98\%$ sensitivity and 100% specificity for patients with $\ge 2$ viable parenchymal or extraparenchymal cysts). Sensitivity drops to 50–60% for a single solitary calcified nodule. Standard ELISA methods have unacceptable false-positive cross-reactions with echinococcosis.
- **Neuroimaging Criteria (The Definitive Diagnostic Anchor):**
  - Contrast-enhanced Brain CT and high-resolution MRI (FIESTA / CISS sequences) categorize lesions based on the Del Brutto Diagnostic Criteria:
    - **Pathognomonic Neuroimaging Sign ("Hole-with-a-Dot"):** A well-circumscribed, small, non-enhancing round cystic lesion (5–20 mm) within the cerebral cortex containing an eccentric, punctate, hyperdense or hyperintense mural nodule representing the **invaginated scolex** (the tapeworm head with hooklets).
    - **Colloidal Stage:** Round, ring-enhancing cystic lesion with thick, regular capsule enhancement and prominent surrounding hypodense vasogenic edema on T2/FLAIR.
    - **Calcified Granuloma Stage:** Single or multiple small (2–5 mm), discrete, hyperdense, non-enhancing punctate calcifications on non-contrast CT, devoid of perilesional edema.
    - **Racemose Cisternal Cysts:** Lobulated, multiseptated cystic masses within the basal subarachnoid spaces without visible scolices, producing ventricular dilation and asymmetric hydrocephalus.

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Cerebral Tuberculoma (*M. tuberculosis*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Ring-enhancing intracranial mass with seizures or focal deficits.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Tuberculomas exhibit thick, irregular, nodular rim enhancement with central hypointensity on T2, often accompanied by systemic constitutional symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss), positive chest X-ray, and negative EITB. NCC is afebrile, thin-walled, exhibits the eccentric scolex dot, and is EITB positive.
- **Pyogenic Brain Abscess:**
  - *Differentiator:* Ring-enhancing lesion with perilesional edema.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Pyogenic brain abscess features acute high fever, toxic delirium, marked neutrophilic leukocytosis, elevated ESR/CRP, and restricted central diffusion (bright signal on DWI with low ADC). Viable NCC lesions do not restrict on DWI, are afebrile, and have normal inflammatory labs.
- **High-Grade Glioma / Brain Metastases:**
  - *Differentiator:* Ring-enhancing cerebral mass with edema in adults.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Glioblastoma and metastatic lesions exhibit thick, irregular, shaggy enhancing walls with necrotic centers, significant mass effect with midline shift, and progressive clinical deterioration. NCC cysts are smooth, discrete, and lack invasive neovascularization.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - A patient with acute high spiking fever, meningeal signs, and purulent CSF pleocytosis rules OUT uncomplicated parenchymal NCC.
  - Large multilocular cystic lesions containing internal daughter cysts or hydatid sand rule OUT neurocysticercosis (points to cerebral echinococcosis).

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **MANDATORY PRINCIPLE: Pre-Treatment Corticosteroid Administration:**
  - **CRITICAL WARNING:** Direct anthelmintic destruction of living cysticerci releases massive foreign parasitic antigens into the cerebral parenchyma, triggering acute local edema, intracranial hypertension, status epilepticus, and death.
  - **RULE:** **Always administer high-dose systemic Corticosteroids (oral Dexamethasone 0.1 mg/kg/day or Prednisone 1 mg/kg/day) starting 24 to 48 hours BEFORE initiating cysticidal therapy, continuing throughout the course and tapering slowly.**
- **Cysticidal Anthelmintic Protocols (Del Brutto Consensus):**
  - **1 to 2 Viable Parenchymal Cysts:** Oral **Albendazole** (15 mg/kg/day divided into 2 doses, taken with high-fat meals) for 10 to 14 days.
  - **Multiple Viable Parenchymal Cysts ($> 2$ Cysts):** **Dual Anthelmintic Therapy** combining oral **Albendazole** (15 mg/kg/day) PLUS oral **Praziquantel** (50 mg/kg/day divided into 3 doses) for 10 to 14 days. Dual therapy achieves significantly superior complete cyst resolution ($>75\%$) compared to monotherapy.
  - **Calcified Inactive Cysts Alone:** Anthelmintic therapy is strictly **CONTRAINDICATED** (the parasites are already dead). Treatment is exclusively anti-epileptic drug (AED) monotherapy (e.g., levetiracetam, carbamazepine) for seizure prophylaxis.
- **Contraindications to Anthelmintics:**
  - **Untreated Hydrocephalus or Elevated ICP:** Anthelmintics must NOT be administered until hydrocephalus is surgically treated via ventriculoperitoneal shunting or endoscopic resection of intraventricular cysts.
  - **Cysticercal Encephalitis:** Massive diffuse parenchymal infection with dozens to hundreds of degenerating cysts causing severe diffuse cerebral edema; anthelmintics are contraindicated; managed solely with high-dose steroids and osmotic agents.
