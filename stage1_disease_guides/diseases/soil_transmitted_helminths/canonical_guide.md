# Soil-Transmitted Helminths - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agents & Taxonomy:** Major intestinal nematode infections transmitted through contact with fecally contaminated soil, affecting >1.5 billion people globally:
  - **1. *Ascaris lumbricoides* (Giant Roundworm):** Largest intestinal nematode (females 20–35 cm, males 15–30 cm). Resides in the lumen of the jejunum and ileum.
  - **2. *Trichuris trichiura* (Whipworm):** Adults (3–5 cm) characterized by a thin, thread-like anterior three-fifths embedded in cecal and colonic epithelial syncytia.
  - **3. Hookworms (*Necator americanus* & *Ancylostoma duodenale*):** Small cylindrical worms (8–13 mm) possessing buccal cutting plates (*Necator*) or ventral teeth (*Ancylostoma*) that latch onto the duodenal and jejunal mucosa.
  - **4. *Strongyloides stercoralis* (Threadworm):** Minute parasitic females (2 mm) capable of an autoinfection cycle permitting lifelong persistence within the human host.
- **Transmission Pathways:**
  - **Fecal-Oral Egg Ingestion:** Ingestion of infective embryonated eggs from contaminated soil, unwashed vegetables/fruits, or unwashed hands (*Ascaris lumbricoides* and *Trichuris trichiura*).
  - **Percutaneous Larval Inoculation:** Free-living infectious **filariform larvae** in warm, moist soil actively penetrate bare skin (typically bare feet, ankles) of humans walking on ground contaminated with feces (*Necator americanus*, *Ancylostoma duodenale*, and *Strongyloides stercoralis*).
- **Geographic Endemicity & Sanitation:** Hyperendemic in rural, impoverished tropical and subtropical communities with inadequate municipal sanitation, lack of latrines, open defecation, and agricultural use of untreated human nightsoil (sub-Saharan Africa, East and South Asia, Latin America).
- **Incubation Periods & Longevity:**
  - Larval pulmonary migration: 1 to 2 weeks post-infection.
  - Prepatent period (time to egg production): *Ascaris* 60–75 days; Hookworms 5–9 weeks; *Trichuris* 60–90 days. Adult lifespans: *Ascaris* 1–2 years; Hookworms 3–7 years; *Trichuris* 1–3 years; *Strongyloides* decades via internal autoinfection.

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
The clinical pathology transitions through larval tissue invasion to chronic intestinal parasite load:
- **1. Acute Tissue Migration Phase (Löffler Syndrome):**
  - Occurs during transpulmonary migration of larvae (*Ascaris*, Hookworms, *Strongyloides*): Larvae break through alveolar capillaries into alveolar spaces.
  - Manifestations: Dry hacking cough, substernal burning chest pain, wheezing, dyspnea, transient urticaria, and **marked peripheral eosinophilia** with fleeting migratory pulmonary infiltrates on radiography.
- **2. Hookworm Disease (Chronic Iron-Deficiency Anemia):**
  - **"Ground Itch":** Intense pruritic, erythematous papular or vesicular dermatitis at the site of larval skin penetration on the feet and interdigital spaces.
  - **Severe Chronic Hypochromic Microcytic Anemia & Hypoalbuminemia:** Adult hookworms attach to small bowel mucosa, lacerate capillaries, secrete anticoagulants, and ingest host blood. Blood loss ranges from **0.03 to 0.05 mL/day per worm (*Necator*) up to 0.2 mL/day per worm (*Ancylostoma*)**.
  - In heavy infections ($> 1,000$ worms), unremitting blood loss exhausts iron and protein stores, leading to profound anemia (hemoglobin dropping to $3–6\text{ g/dL}$), waxy pallor, listlessness, cognitive stunting in children, pica (dirt eating), exertional dyspnea, high-output heart failure, and generalized dependent pitting edema / anasarca.
- **3. *Ascaris lumbricoides* Complications (Mechanical Obstruction):**
  - **Mechanical Intestinal Obstruction:** Heavy worm burdens in young children (masses of 100 to $> 1,000$ coiled worms) aggregate into a solid bolus, mechanically occluding the ileocecal lumen. Presents with colicky abdominal pain, bilious vomiting, obstipation, abdominal distension, and a palpable "doughy", moveable abdominal mass. Can lead to volvulus, intussusception, and bowel gangrene.
  - **Erratic Wandering / Hepatobiliary Ascariasis:** Adult worms stimulated by fever or anesthesia migrate into small orifices: entering the ampulla of Vater to produce severe **biliary colic, acute suppurative cholangitis, acute necrotizing pancreatitis, or hepatic microabscesses**.
- **4. *Trichuris trichiura* (Trichuris Dysentery Syndrome - TDS):**
  - Heavy whipworm burdens ($> 800–1,000$ worms embedded in colonic mucosa) in young children produce TDS:
  - Severe, chronic mucoid bloody diarrhea, excruciating tenesmus, severe microcytic anemia, finger clubbing, and **Pathognomonic Rectal Prolapse** (the hyperemic, edematous rectal mucosa prolapses during defecation, visibly studded with dozens of white whipworms resembling tiny threads).
- **5. *Strongyloides stercoralis* Hallmarks:**
  - **Larva Currens (Pathognomonic Physical Hallmark):** An intensely pruritic, raised, erythematous, serpiginous urticarial linear skin lesion that migrates at an extraordinary speed of **5 to 10 cm per hour** over the perianal skin, buttocks, thighs, and abdomen.
  - **Hyperinfection Syndrome / Dissemination:** Triggered when cell-mediated immunity is suppressed (especially **systemic Corticosteroids** or HTLV-1 infection); larvae hyper-replicate and penetrate the colonic wall into the bloodstream carrying enteric flora, causing polymicrobial Gram-negative bacteremia, septic shock, bacterial meningitis, diffuse alveolar hemorrhage, and ileus (mortality $> 60–80\%$).

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
  - **Severe Microcytic Hypochromic Anemia:** Hemoglobin frequently $< 7\text{ g/dL}$, MCV $< 70\text{ fL}$, low serum ferritin ($< 10\text{ ng/mL}$), and high total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) in hookworm disease.
  - **Peripheral Blood Eosinophilia:** Prominent during tissue migration (Löffler syndrome, $20–50\%$ eosinophils) and chronically present in strongyloidiasis ($10–25\%$). In chronic established luminal *Ascaris* or *Trichuris*, eosinophilia is mild ($5–12\%$) or may be absent.
  - Hypoalbuminemia ($< 2.5\text{ g/dL}$) secondary to protein-losing enteropathy.
- **Stool Microscopy & Parasitology (Gold Standard):**
  - **Kato-Katz Thick Smear / Formalin-Ether Concentration:**
    - *Ascaris lumbricoides:* Large, oval, golden-brown eggs measuring $45–75\ \mu\text{m} \times 35–50\ \mu\text{m}$ with a thick, transparent shell surrounded by a rough, bumpy, mammillated albuminous outer layer.
    - *Trichuris trichiura:* Unmistakable **barrel-shaped or football-shaped eggs** measuring $50–55\ \mu\text{m} \times 22–24\ \mu\text{m}$, golden-brown with a smooth shell and **two prominent, transparent, mucoid bipolar plugs**.
    - Hookworms (*Necator* / *Ancylostoma*): Bluntly rounded, thin-shelled, colorless, transparent oval eggs measuring $60–75\ \mu\text{m} \times 35–40\ \mu\text{m}$ containing a 4-to-8-cell morula.
  - **Diagnostics for *Strongyloides*:** Standard stool exams frequently miss Strongyloides because eggs hatch in the gut. Requires **Baermann funnel concentration**, Koga agar plate culture (larvae leave bacterial tracking trails), or serum IgG ELISA. Stool demonstrates motile **rhabditiform larvae** ($200–300\ \mu\text{m}$).
- **Bedside Imaging (Ultrasound & Abdominal Radiography):**
  - **Abdominal Ultrasound:**
    - *Biliary Ascariasis:* Non-shadowing, linear, hyperechoic tubular structures within the common bile duct with an anechoic central longitudinal line (**"strip sign" / "inner tube sign"**), demonstrating real-time slow curling and writhing movements.
    - *Intestinal Obstruction:* Acoustic shadowing from multiple clustered tubular interfaces forming a mass in bowel loops (**"whirlpool / tangled spaghetti sign"**).
  - **Abdominal Radiography:** Dilated bowel loops with multiple air-fluid levels; barium contrast outlines parallel linear filling defects representing intestinal roundworms (**"railroad track sign"**).

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Nutritional / Non-Parasitic Iron Deficiency Anemia:**
  - *Differentiator:* Microcytic anemia in children or adults.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Nutritional anemia lacks hookworm eggs on stool examination, lacks eosinophilia, and responds permanently to oral iron without deworming. In hookworm areas, anemia rapidly relapses after iron therapy if worms are not cleared.
- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease / Rectal Polyps (vs. Trichuris Dysentery):**
  - *Differentiator:* Bloody diarrhea, tenesmus, rectal prolapse in pediatrics.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* IBD demonstrates transmural or mucosal chronic inflammatory infiltration on colonoscopy without visible parasites. TDS reveals whipworms directly attached to prolapsed rectal mucosa and bipolar-plugged eggs on stool microscopy, resolving rapidly with mebendazole.
- **Scabies / Urticaria (vs. Strongyloides Larva Currens):**
  - *Differentiator:* Pruritic serpiginous lesions on the trunk.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Scabies burrows are stationary, predominantly in finger webs and wrist flexures, with nocturnal pruritus. Larva currens is a hyper-mobile wheal that advances visibly at **5 to 10 cm/hour**.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Absence of parasite eggs or larvae on multiple concentrated stool examinations using Kato-Katz and Baermann methods in an immunocompetent host rules OUT heavy symptomatic soil-transmitted helminthiasis.
  - Normal hemoglobin, normal ferritin, and normal eosinophil count makes heavy hookworm infection virtually impossible.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **First-Line Benzimidazole Anthelmintics (WHO Protocols):**
  - **Albendazole:** Single dose **400 mg** orally (or 200 mg in children 12–24 months). Excellent efficacy against *Ascaris* ($>95\%$ cure) and hookworms ($>80\%$ cure); moderate against *Trichuris*.
  - **Mebendazole:** Single dose **500 mg** orally OR **100 mg twice daily for 3 consecutive days**. Higher efficacy against *Trichuris trichiura*.
- **Ivermectin (Essential Therapy for Strongyloides):**
  - **CRITICAL WARNING:** Benzimidazoles have poor efficacy against *Strongyloides stercoralis*.
  - **Drug of Choice:** Oral **Ivermectin** (200 $\mu$g/kg/day once daily for 2 consecutive days) achieves $>95\%$ cure. In hyperinfection syndrome, daily ivermectin is continued until larvae are eradicated from all bodily secretions for $\ge 2$ weeks.
  - Combination of Albendazole (400 mg) + Ivermectin (200 $\mu$g/kg) is the most potent regimen for refractory *Trichuris trichiura*.
- **Iron Replacement Therapy:** Oral Ferrous sulfate (3–6 mg elemental iron/kg/day in children; 200 mg TID in adults) for 3 months to replenish marrow iron stores after hookworm eradication.
- **Management of Ascaris Intestinal Obstruction:** Conservative management: NPO, nasogastric suction, IV fluids, and oral administration of mineral oil or piperazine citrate (which flaccidly paralyzes worms, allowing them to pass). Laparotomy indicated for complete obstruction, perforation, or peritonitis.
- **Antibiotic Refractoriness:** Unresponsive to standard antibacterial, antiprotozoal, or antifungal therapy.
