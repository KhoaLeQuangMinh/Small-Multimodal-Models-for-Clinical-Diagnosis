# Malaria - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agent & Species:** Protozoan intraerythrocytic parasites of the genus *Plasmodium*:
  - *Plasmodium falciparum:* Responsible for >95% of malaria mortality globally; capable of invading erythrocytes of all ages, leading to high parasitemia and severe microvascular sequestration.
  - *Plasmodium vivax:* Most widespread species outside sub-Saharan Africa (dominant in Asia and Latin America); infects reticulocytes only; forms dormant liver stages (**hypnozoites**) causing late relapses.
  - *Plasmodium ovale:* West/Central Africa; forms dormant hypnozoites.
  - *Plasmodium malariae:* Low-grade chronic infection; infects senescent erythrocytes; causes quartan fever and nephrotic syndrome.
  - *Plasmodium knowlesi:* Zoonotic parasite of Southeast Asia (macaque reservoir); rapid 24-hour replication cycle causing severe illness.
- **Vectors & Transmission:** Transmitted by nocturnal female mosquitoes of the genus *Anopheles* (biting primarily from dusk to dawn). Non-vector transmission occurs via blood transfusions, needle sharing, and vertically (congenital malaria).
- **Geographic Endemicity:** Tropical and subtropical belts: sub-Saharan Africa carries >95% of cases and deaths; also endemic in parts of South and Southeast Asia, Western Pacific, and the Amazon basin.
- **Incubation Periods:**
  - *P. falciparum:* 9–14 days (minimum 7 days; typically 10–12 days).
  - *P. vivax* / *P. ovale:* 12–18 days primary attack, but latent relapses occur from 3–4 weeks (tropical frequent-relapse phenotypes) up to 8–10 months or years (temperate long-latency phenotypes).
  - *P. malariae:* 18–40 days (can persist subclinically for decades).
  - *P. knowlesi:* 9–12 days.

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
- **The Classical Malarial Paroxysm:**
  Corresponds to synchronous schizont rupture and merozoite release into the bloodstream. Follows a characteristic three-stage cycle:
  1. **Cold Stage (15–60 minutes):** Sudden violent teeth-chattering rigors, sensation of intense cold, piloerection, shivering, peripheral vasoconstriction with pale cyanotic nail beds.
  2. **Hot Stage (2–6 hours):** Rapid fever spike to 39–41°C, burning hot and dry skin, severe throbbing headache, nausea, vomiting, epigastric distress, agitation, and delirium.
  3. **Sweating Stage (2–4 hours):** Drenching, profuse diaphoresis, rapid defervescence to normal or subnormal temperature, followed by profound relief and exhaustion/somnolence.
- **Fever Periodicity:**
  - *Tertian (every 48 hours):* *P. vivax*, *P. ovale*, and established *P. falciparum* (though falciparum fever is often continuous or irregular in non-immune patients during the first few days).
  - *Quartan (every 72 hours):* *P. malariae*.
  - *Quotidian (every 24 hours):* *P. knowlesi*.
- **Physical Examination Findings:**
  - Early palpable, soft, tender splenomegaly (develops within 3–7 days of symptoms; can become massive in chronic hyperreactive malaria).
  - Hepatomegaly with mild scleral icterus (hemolytic jaundice).
  - Pallor of conjunctivae, palms, and nail beds due to acute hemolysis.
- **Signs of Severe / Complicated Malaria (*P. falciparum* / *P. knowlesi*):**
  - **Cerebral Malaria:** Coma (Blantyre coma score $< 3$ in children, Glasgow coma score $< 11$ in adults), multiple generalized seizures, abnormal ocular movements, retinal hemorrhages (malaria retinopathy).
  - **Severe Metabolic Acidosis / Respiratory Distress:** Deep, rapid, labored breathing (Kussmaul breathing) secondary to lactic acidosis ($> 5\text{ mmol/L}$) and tissue hypoxia.
  - **Acute Lung Injury / ARDS:** Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema with tachypnea and rales, often precipitated by over-resuscitation.
  - **Acute Kidney Injury (Blackwater Fever):** Oliguria/anuria, elevated serum creatinine, and dark port-wine/black urine caused by massive intravascular hemolysis and hemoglobinuria.
  - **Profound Hypoglycemia:** Blood glucose $< 2.2\text{ mmol/L}$ ($< 40\text{ mg/dL}$), presenting with sudden diaphoresis, tachycardia, and altered consciousness.
  - **Circulatory Collapse ("Algid Malaria"):** Decompensated shock with systolic BP $< 80\text{ mmHg}$, cold extremities, and weak pulse.

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
  - **Hemolytic Anemia:** Normocytic normochromic anemia with rapid drop in hemoglobin and hematocrit; reticulocyte count rises secondary to bone marrow response.
  - **Thrombocytopenia:** Highly prevalent (present in 70–85% of cases), frequently $< 100,000/\mu\text{L}$ and often $< 50,000/\mu\text{L}$, driven by splenic pooling and immune destruction; occurs independently of DIC.
  - **WBC Count:** Normal or leukopenic (WBC $3,000–6,000/\mu\text{L}$). Marked leukocytosis ($> 12,000/\mu\text{L}$) strongly suggests concurrent severe bacterial sepsis or impending mortality.
- **Hemolysis & Metabolic Chemistry:**
  - **Unconjugated (Indirect) Hyperbilirubinemia:** Elevated indirect bilirubin with mild transaminitis ($< 3\times$ ULN), reflecting extravascular and intravascular erythrocyte lysis.
  - Markedly elevated Serum Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) and absent/depleted haptoglobin.
  - Lactic acidosis (plasma lactate $> 5\text{ mmol/L}$, base deficit $> 10\text{ mEq/L}$, serum bicarbonate $< 15\text{ mEq/L}$).
  - Hypoglycemia ($< 2.2\text{ mmol/L}$).
  - Elevated serum creatinine ($> 3.0\text{ mg/dL}$) in renal compromise.
- **Diagnostic Confirmation at Triage:**
  - **Peripheral Blood Smear (Giemsa Stain):** Thick smear for high-sensitivity parasite detection; thin smear for species identification and parasitemia quantification. Parasitemia $> 2\%$ in non-immune individuals (or $> 100,000/\mu\text{L}$, or $> 10\%$ in semi-immune) denotes hyperparasitemia / severe disease. Ring-form trophozoites, banana-shaped gametocytes (*P. falciparum*), or Schüffner dots / amoeboid trophozoites (*P. vivax*).
  - **Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs):** Detection of *P. falciparum* histidine-rich protein 2 (HRP2) and pan-Plasmodium parasite lactate dehydrogenase (pLDH) or aldolase.
- **Bedside Imaging (POCUS & Chest X-ray):**
  - Splenomegaly and mild hepatomegaly without focal mass or abscess.
  - Normal gallbladder wall without striated thickening (distinguishes from Dengue).
  - Chest X-ray shows clear lung fields initially; bilateral diffuse infiltrates indicate ARDS.

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Dengue:**
  - *Differentiator:* Dengue causes severe retro-orbital pain, early cutaneous flushing, positive tourniquet test, hemoconcentration (hematocrit rise $\ge 20\%$), plasma leakage (pleural effusion, ascites), and AST > ALT elevation.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Dengue features leukopenia and hemoconcentration without hemolytic jaundice (elevated indirect bilirubin) or splenomegaly. Malaria causes hemolytic anemia and splenomegaly without microvascular plasma leakage.
- **Typhoid Fever (*Salmonella* Typhi):**
  - *Differentiator:* Step-ladder gradual fever escalation, relative bradycardia (Faget sign), rose spots on trunk, dull apathetic facies, hepatosplenomegaly, and absence of paroxysmal shaking rigors.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Malaria presents with violent abrupt teeth-chattering rigors and drenching sweats; typhoid has continuous fever without distinct sweating paroxysms.
- **Leptospirosis:**
  - *Differentiator:* Marked bilateral conjunctival suffusion without discharge, severe calf muscle tenderness, direct conjugated hyperbilirubinemia with deep jaundice, and marked neutrophilic leukocytosis.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Marked leukocytosis (WBC $> 15,000/\mu\text{L}$) with high ESR/CRP favors Leptospirosis over uncomplicated Malaria.
- **Scrub Typhus:**
  - *Differentiator:* Black necrotic cigarette-burn eschar, regional lymphadenopathy, macular trunk rash, and outdoor mite habitat exposure.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Onset of symptoms within $< 7$ days after first entering an endemic area rules OUT *P. falciparum* malaria.
  - Three consecutive quality-assured thick and thin blood smears and negative RDTs taken 12–24 hours apart completely rules OUT acute malaria.
  - Presence of plasma leakage (pleural effusion, ascites, hemoconcentration) rules OUT uncomplicated malaria.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **Rapid Artemisinin Efficacy:**
  - Uncomplicated malaria responds dramatically to oral **Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT)** (artemether-lumefantrine, artesunate-amodiaquine, dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine) with complete defervescence and $>90\%$ parasite clearance within 24–48 hours.
  - Severe malaria responds rapidly to intravenous **Artesunate** (2.4 mg/kg IV at 0, 12, 24 hours, then daily), which significantly reduces mortality compared to intravenous quinine and clears circulating ring-stage parasitemia swiftly.
- **Radical Hypnozoite Clearance:**
  - *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* require **Primaquine** (0.25–0.5 mg/kg daily for 14 days) or single-dose **Tafenoquine** after checking G6PD enzymatic activity (to avoid life-threatening acute intravascular hemolysis in G6PD-deficient patients).
- **Antibiotic Refractoriness:** Empirical beta-lactams, cephalosporins, or aminoglycosides produce zero defervescence or reduction in parasitemia.
