# Zika Virus - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Geographic Endemicity & Vectors:** Tropical and subtropical Americas, Caribbean, Pacific Islands, Southeast Asia, and parts of Africa. Transmitted primarily by diurnal female *Aedes aegypti* and *Aedes albopictus* mosquitoes (aggressive daytime biters, peaking in early morning and late afternoon).
- **Secondary Transmission Modes:** Uniquely among major arboviruses, Zika virus undergoes non-vector human-to-human transmission:
  - **Sexual Transmission:** Viable virus sheds prolonged in semen (up to several months post-infection), vaginal secretions, and saliva.
  - **Vertical / Congenital Transmission:** Transplacental transmission causing Congenital Zika Syndrome across all trimesters, with highest risk of severe embryopathy in the first trimester.
  - **Blood Products & Organ Transplantation:** Direct hematogenous spread.
- **Incubation Period:** 3–14 days (typically 3–7 days).
- **Population Vulnerability:** High risk in pregnant women and travel-exposed individuals; up to 75–80% of human infections remain asymptomatic or subclinical.

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
The acute clinical syndrome is typically mild, benign, and self-limiting, persisting for 2–7 days:
- **Cardinal Clinical Tetrad:**
  1. **Low-Grade Fever:** Typically mild ($37.5^\circ\text{C}–38.5^\circ\text{C}$), non-spiking, or completely absent (unlike the high rigors of dengue or malaria).
  2. **Maculopapular Pruritic Rash:** Erythematous, punctate, finely papular, frequently pruritic; typically starts on the face on day 1–2 of illness and rapidly spreads centrifugally to trunk, extremities, palms, and soles.
  3. **Bilateral Non-Purulent Conjunctivitis:** Diffuse bulbar and palpebral conjunctival hyperemia without exudate, pus, or photophobia.
  4. **Arthralgia & Periarticular Edema:** Mild to moderate migratory polyarthralgia primarily involving small joints of the hands, wrists, knees, and ankles, often accompanied by soft tissue periarticular puffiness or tenosynovitis.
- **Associated Symptoms:** Retro-orbital ache (mild compared to dengue), malaise, headache, myalgia, anorexia, and posterior cervical lymphadenopathy.
- **Neurological & Teratogenic Complications:**
  - **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):** Acute ascending flaccid paralysis developing 1–3 weeks post-infection due to post-infectious autoimmune polyradiculoneuropathy. Also myelitis, meningoencephalitis, and cranial neuropathies.
  - **Congenital Zika Syndrome (CZS):** Severe microcephaly, cerebral parenchymal calcifications (corticomedullary junction), ventriculomegaly, macular chorioretinal atrophy, and arthrogryposis (congenital joint contractures).

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
  - Normal or minimally depressed total white blood cell count (mild leukopenia, WBC $3,500–5,000/\mu\text{L}$); severe leukopenia ($< 2,000/\mu\text{L}$) is atypical.
  - Normal or mild thrombocytopenia (platelets typically $100,000–150,000/\mu\text{L}$); platelets dropping below $50,000/\mu\text{L}$ is extremely rare in uncomplicated Zika.
  - Normal hematocrit (HCT) without evidence of hemoconcentration or vascular permeability.
- **Liver & Renal Panels:**
  - Generally normal or mild transient elevations of AST and ALT ($< 2\times$ ULN).
  - Serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, and urinalysis are normal.
  - Acute phase reactants (CRP, ESR) are mildly elevated or normal.
- **Bedside Imaging:**
  - **Bedside POCUS:** Spleen, liver, and gallbladder wall are completely normal. Complete absence of pleural effusion, ascites, or pericardial fluid (differentiating from Dengue).
  - **Neuroimaging (in congenital CZS / GBS):** Head ultrasound or CT in newborns demonstrates cortical thinning, subcortical calcifications, and simplified gyral pattern.

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Dengue Virus:**
  - *Differentiator:* Dengue causes severe incapacitating retro-orbital headache, high continuous/biphasic fever ($> 39^\circ\text{C}$), severe prostration, profound thrombocytopenia ($< 50,000/\mu\text{L}$), hemoconcentration, and plasma leakage.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Presence of plasma leakage (pleural effusion, ascites, hematocrit rise $\ge 20\%$), severe thrombocytopenia, or spontaneous mucosal hemorrhage completely rules OUT uncomplicated Zika virus.
- **Chikungunya Virus:**
  - *Differentiator:* Chikungunya causes abrupt, high-spiking fever and crippling, severe, symmetric, inflammatory polyarthritis (wrists, interphalangeal joints, ankles) that often persists for months to years with tenosynovitis.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Inability to ambulate or bend fingers due to severe joint pain with morning stiffness strongly favors Chikungunya over Zika.
- **Rubella & Measles:**
  - *Differentiator:* Measles presents with high continuous fever, prominent cough, coryza, severe photophobic conjunctivitis, and Koplik spots on buccal mucosa. Rubella presents with prominent retroauricular and suboccipital lymphadenopathy.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Presence of cough, purulent nasal discharge, high fever, or Koplik spots rules OUT Zika virus.
- **Parvovirus B19:**
  - *Differentiator:* Causes classic "slapped-cheek" facial rash in pediatrics, or acute symmetrical polyarthropathy mimicking rheumatoid arthritis in adult females, with no tropical vector or geographical requirement.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Persistent fever lasting $> 7$ days rules OUT acute Zika.
  - Purulent ocular discharge or pseudomembranes rules OUT Zika conjunctivitis (points to bacterial or adenoviral conjunctivitis).
  - Hypotension, narrow pulse pressure, or hypovolemic shock rules OUT uncomplicated Zika.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **Antipyretics & Analgesia:** Symptomatic relief with acetaminophen (paracetamol). NSAIDs and aspirin are generally avoided until dengue has been clinically excluded due to potential bleeding diathesis.
- **Antihistamines:** Second-generation oral antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine) provide relief for pruritic cutaneous eruptions.
- **Antibiotic Refractoriness:** Completely unresponsive to empirical antibiotics (beta-lactams, fluoroquinolones, macrolides).
