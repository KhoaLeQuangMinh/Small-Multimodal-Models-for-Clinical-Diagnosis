# Scabies - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agent & Biology:** Caused by *Sarcoptes scabiei* var. *hominis*, an obligate, microscopic, human ectoparasitic mite belonging to the class Arachnida (subclass Acari). Adult females measure 0.3 to 0.45 mm in length; adult males measure 0.2 mm.
  - The fertilized female mite uses proteolytic secretions to excavate and tunnel into the stratum corneum of the epidermis at a rate of 0.5 to 5 mm per day, feeding on dissolved host intercellular fluid.
  - The female deposits 2 to 3 eggs per day along with dark brown fecal pellets (**scybala**) within the burrow over her 4- to 6-week lifespan.
- **Transmission Pathways:**
  - **Classical Scabies:** Direct, prolonged, skin-to-skin contact (typically requiring $\ge 10–20$ minutes of continuous physical contact, such as holding hands, sleeping in the same bed, or sexual contact). Fomite transmission (bedding, clothing, shared towels) is inefficient in classical scabies because the total body burden of viable adult female mites is typically small (average 10 to 15 mites per host).
  - **Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies:** Extremely contagious; the skin harbors **millions of live mites** within thick exfoliative crusts. Transmission occurs readily through brief casual touch or indirect contamination via shed environmental dust, clothing, and bedding.
- **Global Burden & High-Risk Settings:** Affects >200 million people globally at any time. Hyperendemic in overcrowded, low-resource communities: remote tropical islands of the South Pacific, Indigenous communities in northern Australia (prevalence up to 50%), sub-Saharan Africa, urban slums, refugee camps, prisons, day-care centers, and long-term residential aged care facilities.
- **Incubation Period & Sensitization Timeline:**
  - **Primary Infection (First Exposure):** Clinical manifestations are delayed by an asymptomatic incubation period of **3 to 6 weeks** required to mount a Type IV cell-mediated delayed hypersensitivity reaction against mite proteins, saliva, and scybala.
  - **Re-infection:** Sensitized individuals develop intense pruritus and inflammatory lesions within **24 to 48 hours** of re-exposure.

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
The clinical diagnosis is defined according to the 2020 International Alliance for the Control of Scabies (IACS) Consensus Criteria:
- **1. Cardinal Clinical Hallmark (Nocturnal Pruritus):**
  - **Intense, Disproportionate Pruritus:** Severe, unrelenting itching that characteristically **worsens dramatically at night** (disturbing sleep) and following warm baths or showers.
- **2. The Pathognomonic Scabietic Burrow ("Smoking Gun"):**
  - Pathognomonic physical sign present in 40–70% of classical presentations.
  - Appears as a delicate, thread-like, thin, slightly elevated, wavy or serpiginous, grayish-white or brownish intraepidermal line measuring **1 to 10 mm in length**.
  - Terminates in a tiny, discrete, pinpoint papule or vesicle harboring the living female mite.
- **3. Anatomical Predilection Sites (The Classic Adult Distribution):**
  - **Interdigital Web Spaces of the Hands:** The single most frequent anatomical site of burrows and primary papules.
  - **Flexor Creases of the Wrists** and ulnar borders of the forearms.
  - Anterior axillary folds and posterior axillary borders.
  - Peri-umbilical skin, waistline, and beltline.
  - Buttocks, inferior gluteal folds, and upper posterior thighs.
  - **Male Genitalia (Pathognomonic Hallmark):** Exquisitely pruritic, firm, indurated, erythematous or violaceous inflammatory **nodules on the penile shaft, glans, and scrotum** (nodular scabies representing delayed hypersensitivity, often persisting for months after cure).
  - Areolae and infra-mammary creases in females.
- **4. The "Rule of Sparing" in Adults vs. Infantile Scabies:**
  - **Adult Immunocompetent Pattern:** The **head, face, scalp, neck, palms, and soles are characteristically SPARED**.
  - **Infantile Scabies (The Exception):** In infants under 2 years of age, the classic distribution breaks down; burrows, vesicles, and pustules frequently involve the **scalp, face, neck, retroauricular folds, palms of the hands, and soles of the feet**, frequently presenting as diffuse infantile acropustulosis.
- **5. Crusted Scabies (Norwegian Scabies - Hyperinfestation):**
  - Develops in hosts with impaired cell-mediated immunity: advanced HIV/AIDS, HTLV-1 coinfection, solid organ transplant recipients, systemic corticosteroid therapy, or severe cognitive/physical debility.
  - Massive, thick, adherent, fissured, yellow-gray or brown hyperkeratotic crusts and scales resembling severe psoriasis or Darier's disease, involving extensive areas of the hands, feet, scalp, and subungual nail beds (onychauxis).
  - Pruritus may be mild or completely **absent**.
- **6. Secondary Bacterial Superinfections & Systemic Sequelae:**
  - Intense excoriation breaks the epidermal barrier, leading to secondary infection by *Streptococcus pyogenes* (Group A Streptococcus - GAS) and *Staphylococcus aureus*: impetigo, ecthyma, cellulitis, and furunculosis.
  - In tropical endemic populations, scabies-driven GAS pyoderma is a leading trigger of **Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)**, acute rheumatic fever, and rheumatic heart disease.

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Diagnostic Confirmation at Triage (2020 IACS Consensus Criteria):**
  - **Level A: Confirmed Scabies (Direct Parasite Visualization):**
    - **A1: Microscopic Examination of Skin Scrapings:** Superficial epidermal scraping of a burrow using a sterile scalpel blade lubricated with mineral oil, placed on a glass slide: direct microscopic demonstration of the **adult mite (0.3–0.4 mm with 4 pairs of legs), viable oval eggs (100–150 $\mu$m), or dark brown fecal pellets (scybala)** under 10–40$\times$ magnification.
    - **A2: High-Resolution Dermoscopy (The Bedside Gold Standard):** Examination of a burrow at 10–20$\times$ magnification reveals the pathognomonic **"Delta-Wing Jet / Hang-Glider Sign"** (a dark, pigmented, triangular structure representing the anterior head and first two pairs of legs of the mite) located at the terminus of a serpentine intraepidermal burrow (**"jet with vapor contrail"**).
  - **Level B: Clinical Scabies:**
    - Scabietic burrows on physical exam; OR typical lesions on male genitalia; OR typical lesions in a characteristic distribution plus two history features (nocturnal itch + household contact with identical itch).
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Immunology:**
  - CBC is typically normal in classical scabies.
  - Mild peripheral blood eosinophilia ($5–15\%$) is present in 20–30% of patients.
  - Massive eosinophilia ($> 30–50\%$) and marked elevation of total serum IgE ($> 1,000–10,000\text{ IU/mL}$) are characteristic of Crusted Scabies.

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Atopic Dermatitis / Eczema:**
  - *Differentiator:* Intense pruritus, excoriated papules.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Atopic eczema involves the antecubital and popliteal flexures, neck, and face; lacks linear burrows; lacks penile/scrotal nodules; lacks the delta-wing sign on dermoscopy; and does NOT spread simultaneously to close household contacts.
- **Insect Bites / Papular Urticaria:**
  - *Differentiator:* Pruritic erythematous papules and wheals.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Insect bites are clustered on exposed limbs ("breakfast, lunch, and dinner" pattern) and lack burrows, web-space involvement, or genital nodules.
- **Plaque Psoriasis (vs. Crusted Scabies):**
  - *Differentiator:* Hyperkeratotic scaly plaques.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Psoriasis produces silvery, micaceous scales on extensor surfaces (elbows, knees) with the Auspitz sign (punctate bleeding upon removal of scale), and scraping shows zero mites. Crusted scabies scrapings show thousands of viable mites, eggs, and scybala.
- **Secondary Syphilis (*Treponema pallidum*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Maculopapular rash involving palms and soles.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Secondary syphilis lesions are non-pruritic, accompanied by generalized lymphadenopathy and condyloma lata, with positive treponemal serology.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Total absence of pruritus in an immunocompetent host virtually rules OUT classical scabies.
  - A pruritic eruption that involves the face and scalp in an adult immunocompetent individual rules OUT classical scabies.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **First-Line Scabicidal Therapies:**
  - **1. Topical 5% Permethrin Cream (The Gold Standard):**
    - Synthetic pyrethroid causing neurotoxic paralysis of mites by disrupting sodium channels:
    - **Application Protocol:** Applied thoroughly from the neck down to the soles of the feet, paying meticulous attention to finger webs, wrists, under fingernails (use a brush), axillae, umbilicus, and genitalia. Left on for **8 to 14 hours overnight** before washing off.
    - **MANDATORY SECOND DOSE:** **A second application 7 to 14 days later is MANDATORY.** Permethrin kills active mites and larvae, but a fraction of eggs survive; the second application eradicates newly hatched larvae before they reach sexual maturity.
  - **2. Oral Ivermectin (Systemic Therapy):**
    - First-line oral treatment: **Ivermectin** 200 $\mu$g/kg as a single dose taken with water.
    - **MANDATORY SECOND DOSE:** Must be repeated **7 to 14 days later** due to lack of ovicidal activity.
    - Drug of choice for institutional outbreaks (nursing homes, prisons) and Mass Drug Administration (MDA) programs in endemic communities.
    - Safe in children $\ge 15\text{ kg}$; contraindicated in pregnancy and children $< 15\text{ kg}$ (use topical 5% permethrin or 5–10% sulfur ointment).
- **Protocol for Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies (Intensive Combination):**
  - Requires simultaneous combination therapy: Daily topical 5% Permethrin cream for 1–2 weeks PLUS multiple doses of oral Ivermectin (200 $\mu$g/kg given on days 1, 2, 8, 9, 15, and on days 22 and 29 for severe refractory crusting), combined with topical keratolytics (5% salicylic acid or 40% urea) to dissolve thick protective scales.
- **THE GOLDEN RULE OF HOUSEHOLD CONTACTS:**
  - **Simultaneous treatment of ALL household members and sexual contacts is MANDATORY**, regardless of whether they have symptoms (because asymptomatic contacts harbor incubation-phase mites).
  - Decontamination: Clothing, bedding, and towels used within the preceding 3 days must be washed in hot water ($> 60^\circ\text{C}$) and dried on high heat, or sealed in airtight plastic bags for 72 hours (mites dehydrate and die within 48–72 hours away from human skin).
- **Post-Scabietic Pruritus:** Itching characteristically persists for **2 to 4 weeks** after successful curative treatment due to lingering dead parasite antigens. It does NOT indicate treatment failure and should be managed with emollients, mild topical corticosteroids, and oral antihistamines.
