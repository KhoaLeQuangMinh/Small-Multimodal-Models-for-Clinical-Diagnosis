# Trypanosomiasis - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agents & Geographic Duality:**
  Caused by kinetoplastid hemoflagellates of the genus *Trypanosoma*, dividing into two fundamentally distinct continental diseases:
  - **1. American Trypanosomiasis (Chagas Disease):** Caused by *Trypanosoma cruzi*. Endemic in 21 Latin American countries (affecting 6–8 million individuals, particularly in rural Mexico, Central America, Bolivia, Brazil, and northern Argentina). Non-endemic cases occur globally via human migration (United States, Europe).
  - **2. Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT / Sleeping Sickness):** Confined to sub-Saharan Africa across 36 countries:
    - *Trypanosoma brucei gambiense* (West and Central Africa, accounting for >95% of cases): Anthroponotic, chronic, slowly progressive infection.
    - *Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense* (Eastern and Southern Africa, accounting for <5% of cases): Zoonotic (wild game and domestic cattle reservoirs), acute, rapidly fulminant illness.
- **Vectors & Transmission Pathways:**
  - **Chagas Disease:**
    - Transmitted by nocturnal hematophagous triatomine bugs (family Reduviidae, subfamily Triatominae; "kissing bugs", *Triatoma infestans*, *Rhodnius prolixus*). The vector defecates on host skin while feeding; metacyclic trypomastigotes are rubbed into the bite wound or mucous membranes (eyes/mouth).
    - **Oral Transmission:** Ingestion of fresh, unpasteurized juices (açai, sugar cane juice) contaminated with crushed infected triatomines, producing an explosive, severe acute myocarditis outbreak.
    - Congenital (transplacental), blood transfusion, organ transplant, and laboratory accidents.
  - **African Trypanosomiasis:**
    - Transmitted by the painful daytime bite of female and male **tsetse flies** (genus *Glossina*): *G. palpalis* group (riverine habitats near water) for *T. b. gambiense*; *G. morsitans* group (savannah and woodlands) for *T. b. rhodesiense*.
- **Incubation Periods:**
  - Chagas Disease: Vector-borne 1–2 weeks; oral ingestion 3–22 days. Indeterminate latency spans 10 to 30 years.
  - African Trypanosomiasis: *T. b. rhodesiense* 1–3 weeks; *T. b. gambiense* months to several years.

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
- **1. American Trypanosomiasis (Chagas Disease):**
  - **Acute Phase (Weeks 1–8):**
    - High parasitemia, usually mild or subclinical, but can manifest with prolonged fever, hepatosplenomegaly, and lymphadenopathy.
    - **Inoculation Chagoma:** Inflammatory, indurated, violaceous skin nodule at the site of triatomine bug defecation.
    - **Romaña Sign (Pathognomonic Physical Hallmark):** Present in up to 50% of acute ocular inoculations; **unilateral, painless, bipalpebral periorbital edema and violaceous erythema** accompanied by ipsilateral conjunctivitis and non-tender preauricular lymphadenopathy.
    - Acute myocarditis (tachycardia, cardiomegaly, pericardial effusion) or meningoencephalitis in young children.
  - **Indeterminate Phase (10–30 Years):** Asymptomatic seropositive latency with normal ECG and radiographic findings.
  - **Chronic Phase (Develops in 30–40% of Patients Decades Later):**
    - **Chronic Chagasic Cardiomyopathy (CCC):**
      - Dilated cardiomyopathy with biventricular failure (dyspnea, peripheral edema, jugular venous distension).
      - **Apical Left Ventricular Aneurysm:** Pathognomonic thinning, dyskinesia, or outpouching of the LV apex, predisposing to intra-aneurysmal mural thrombus and catastrophic systemic thromboembolic stroke.
      - **Conduction System Destruction:** Syncope, lightheadedness, and sudden cardiac death secondary to **Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB) combined with Left Anterior Fascicular Block (LAFB)**, complete heart block, or ventricular tachycardia.
    - **Digestive Megasyndromes (Chagasic Megadisease):**
      - Destruction of myenteric plexus parasympathetic neurons (Auerbach and Meissner plexuses):
      - **Megaesophagus:** Progressive severe dysphagia for solids and liquids, painful odynophagia, regurgitation of undigested food, nocturnal aspiration, chronic cough, and severe cachexia.
      - **Megacolon:** Intractable chronic obstipation (bowel movements only once every 2 to 4 weeks), massive meteorism, abdominal distension, impaction of stony hard **fecalomas**, and sigmoid volvulus requiring emergency surgery.
- **2. Human African Trypanosomiasis (Sleeping Sickness):**
  - **Stage 1: Hemo-Lymphatic Stage:**
    - **Trypanosomal Chancre:** Painful, tender, indurated erythematous nodule at the tsetse bite site appearing within days, ulcerating with central desquamation.
    - Intermittent, irregular fevers, severe headache, pruritus, arthralgia, and transient circinate erythematous rash.
    - **Winterbottom Sign (Pathognomonic Physical Hallmark):** Striking, visible, non-tender, rubbery, mobile enlargement of the **posterior cervical lymph nodes** (posterior triangle of the neck), classic in *T. b. gambiense*.
  - **Stage 2: Meningo-Encephalitic Stage (CNS Invasion):**
    - Trypanosomes cross the blood-brain barrier into the central nervous system.
    - **Pathognomonic Circadian Sleep Disruption:** Reversal of the normal sleep-wake cycle: daytime uncontrollable hypersomnia and sleep attacks, alternating with intractable nocturnal insomnia and motor restlessness.
    - Progressive neuropsychiatric decay: apathy, aggressive personality changes, severe dementia, tremors, choreiform movements, ataxia, parkinsonian rigidity, and progressive terminal coma ("sleeping sickness").

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Diagnostic Confirmation at Triage:**
  - **Acute Chagas Disease:** Direct visualization of motile, C-shaped flagellated *T. cruzi* trypomastigotes on fresh anticoagulated blood wet mounts, microhematocrit tube centrifugation (Woo test), or Giemsa-stained thin/thick smears.
  - **Chronic Chagas Disease:** Direct microscopy is negative due to subpatent parasitemia. Requires **two positive serological assays** utilizing different antigenic preparations (e.g., ELISA and indirect immunofluorescence).
  - **Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT):**
    - Stage 1: Card Agglutination Test for Trypanosomiasis (CATT) for *T. b. gambiense*; microscopic visualization of extracellular trypomastigotes on Giemsa blood smears or lymph node aspirates.
    - Stage 2 (Mandatory Lumbar Puncture for Staging): CSF examination demonstrates elevated protein ($> 45\text{ mg/dL}$), lymphocytic pleocytosis ($> 5\text{ WBC/}\mu\text{L}$), or visualization of trypanosomes in centrifuged CSF. Presence of **Mott cells** (morular plasma cells containing IgM inclusions) is characteristic of Stage 2.
- **Cardiovascular & Imaging Hallmarks of Chronic Chagas:**
  - **Electrocardiography (ECG):** Characterized by **Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB) plus Left Anterior Fascicular Block (LAFB)** in $>50\%$ of chronic cardiac patients; multifocal premature ventricular complexes (PVCs) and high-degree AV block.
  - **Echocardiography:** Segmental wall motion abnormalities, severe global LV systolic impairment, and pathognomonic **left ventricular apical aneurysm** with mural thrombus.
  - **Barium Radiography:**
    - Esophagogram: Dilation of esophageal lumen, delayed emptying, and distal tapering (**"bird-beak sign"** or pencil-tip narrowing) mimicking idiopathic achalasia.
    - Barium Enema: Enormous elongation and dilatation of the sigmoid colon and rectum (dolichomegacolon).

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Idiopathic Dilated Cardiomyopathy / Ischemic Heart Disease:**
  - *Differentiator:* Biventricular failure, cardiomegaly.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy causes diffuse global hypokinesia without apical thinning. Chagas cardiomyopathy features localized apical aneurysm, RBBB + LAFB on ECG, digestive mega-syndromes, Latin American rural exposure, and positive *T. cruzi* serology.
- **Idiopathic Achalasia / Toxic Megacolon:**
  - *Differentiator:* Esophageal dysphagia or severe colonic distension.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Idiopathic achalasia lacks concurrent apical cardiac aneurysms, bundle branch conduction blocks, and triatomine exposure.
- **Malaria / Visceral Leishmaniasis (vs. African Trypanosomiasis):**
  - *Differentiator:* Splenomegaly, prolonged fever, African travel.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Malaria features intraerythrocytic parasites on blood smear; visceral leishmaniasis features profound pancytopenia and intracellular amastigotes. African trypanosomiasis features Winterbottom cervical adenopathy, extra-erythrocytic flagellates in wet mounts, and marked daytime sleep disruption.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Absence of travel or residence in Latin America completely rules OUT Chagas disease (except rare congenital/blood transfusion transmissions).
  - A completely normal 12-lead ECG and normal echocardiogram virtually rules OUT symptomatic Chagas cardiomyopathy.
  - Normal CSF cell count ($< 5\text{ WBC/}\mu\text{L}$) and absent CSF trypanosomes rules OUT Stage 2 meningoencephalitic African sleeping sickness.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **Antiparasitic Therapy for Chagas Disease:**
  - **Acute Phase / Congenital / Early Chronic:** Oral **Benznidazole** (5–7 mg/kg/day divided in 2 doses for 60 days) or oral **Nifurtimox** (8–10 mg/kg/day divided in 3 doses for 60–90 days). Highly effective in acute infection ($>80\%$ parasitological cure); halts progression if administered early. Common adverse effects include severe exfoliative dermatitis, peripheral neuropathy, and gastrointestinal intolerance.
  - **Advanced Chronic Cardiomyopathy:** Benznidazole does not reverse established apical fibrosis or mega-syndromes; management focuses on heart failure regimens, antiarrhythmics (amiodarone), pacemakers/ICDs, and surgical resection of megacolon.
- **Antiparasitic Protocols for Human African Trypanosomiasis:**
  - ***T. b. gambiense* (West African):**
    - **Stage 1 (Hemolymphatic):** Intramuscular **Pentamidine** isethionate (4 mg/kg/day for 7–10 days).
    - **Stage 2 (CNS):** Oral **Fexinidazole** (for non-severe Stage 2) OR **NECT** (Nifurtimox-Eflornithine Combination Therapy: oral nifurtimox 15 mg/kg/day for 10 days plus IV eflornithine 400 mg/kg/day for 7 days), achieving $>95\%$ cure with low toxicity.
  - ***T. b. rhodesiense* (East African):**
    - **Stage 1:** Intravenous **Suramin** (20 mg/kg up to 1 g weekly for 5 doses).
    - **Stage 2:** Intravenous **Melarsoprol** (toxic organoarsenical; causes fatal post-treatment reactive encephalopathy in 5–10% of patients).
- **Antibiotic Refractoriness:** Completely insensitive to standard antibacterial and antiviral agents.
