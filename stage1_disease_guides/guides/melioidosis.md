# Melioidosis - Canonical Clinical Profile

## 1. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL EXPOSURES & TRANSMISSION
- **Etiologic Agent & Environmental Reservoirs:** Caused by *Burkholderia pseudomallei*, an aerobic, motile, oxidase-positive, Gram-negative saprophytic bacillus exhibiting a characteristic bipolar "safety-pin" staining appearance. It persists as a free-living organism in tropical wet soils, muddy surface waters, and agricultural fields.
- **Geographic Endemicity:** Hyperendemic in northern Australia (Top End, Kimberley) and Southeast Asia (northeast Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos). Increasingly identified as endemic in South Asia (India, Sri Lanka), the tropical Americas (Brazil, Caribbean), and parts of sub-Saharan Africa.
- **Transmission Pathways:**
  - Percutaneous inoculation: Penetration of broken skin or minor abrasions through direct exposure to contaminated wet soil, mud, or surface water (e.g., rice farming, walking barefoot, gardening, construction).
  - Inhalation: Inhalation of aerosolized soil and droplets during severe tropical weather, monsoons, cyclones, and flooding.
  - Ingestion: Consumption of untreated, unchlorinated surface drinking water.
  - Rare person-to-person spread (sexual transmission, peripartum, breast milk) or accidental laboratory exposure.
- **Crucial Host Predispositions ("The Diabetogenic Infection"):**
  - Up to 80% of melioidosis patients have recognizable chronic underlying conditions:
    - **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** The single strongest risk factor, present in $>40–60\%$ of all patients (diabetic neutrophil oxidative burst dysfunction).
    - Hazardous alcohol consumption (15–30%).
    - Chronic kidney disease (10–25%).
    - Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or cystic fibrosis.
    - Thalassemia and immunosuppressive corticosteroid therapy.
  - Presentation in an immunocompetent individual without soil/water exposure is exceedingly rare.
- **Incubation Period & Latency:**
  - Acute disease: 1–21 days (mean 9 days; as short as 24–48 hours following major severe weather / near-drowning events).
  - Latent disease: Can remain dormant within phagocytic cells for months, years, or decades ("the Vietnam Time-Bomb" reactivating up to 60 years post-exposure when immunosuppression develops).

## 2. CARDINAL CLINICAL SIGNS & TIMELINE
Known as the "Great Mimicker" due to protean clinical manifestations ranging from localized abscesses to fulminant bacteremic septic shock:
- **1. Acute Pulmonary Melioidosis (50% of Cases):**
  - Most common clinical presentation.
  - Ranges from mild subacute bronchitis to acute necrotizing lobar consolidation or cavitary pneumonia.
  - Presentation: High spiking fever, profuse drenching sweats, productive purulent cough, pleuritic chest pain, and hemoptysis. Upper lobe cavitary lesions closely mimic active pulmonary tuberculosis.
- **2. Deep Visceral & Multi-Organ Abscesses (Pathognomonic Organ Distributions):**
  - **Prostatic Abscesses:** Present in 18–30% of adult males in endemic cohorts (especially northern Australia). Characterized by high fever, perineal/pelvic pain, dysuria, hesitancy, and acute urinary retention.
  - **Splenic & Hepatic Abscesses:** Characterized by multiple small, discrete, coalescing microabscesses throughout the parenchyma, creating a pathognomonic **"honeycomb" or "Swiss cheese" appearance** on cross-sectional imaging.
  - **Suppurative Parotitis:** Hallmark manifestation in pediatric cohorts (predominantly in Thailand and Cambodia, representing up to 30% of pediatric cases); presents with acute, painful, unilateral preauricular swelling and abscess formation.
  - **Skin & Soft Tissue Inoculation:** Indolent non-healing ulcers, erythematous nodules, furuncles, carbuncles, or cellulitis with rapid regional abscess formation and lymphadenopathy.
  - **Musculoskeletal:** Acute suppurative monoarthritis (knee, hip, ankle) or osteomyelitis.
  - **Neurological Melioidosis (Encephalomyelitis):** Distinctive syndrome involving brainstem encephalitis, unilateral cranial nerve palsies (especially CN VII), flaccid quadriparesis, or cerebellar ataxia, often with normal initial neuroimaging.
- **3. Acute Septicemic Shock:**
  - Fulminant bacteremic dissemination with hypothermia or hyperpyrexia, profound refractory hypotension, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), and multi-organ failure. Untreated mortality exceeds 90%; treated mortality ranges from 10–15% (Australia) to 40% (Thailand).

## 3. ADMISSION LAB & BEDSIDE IMAGING HALLMARKS
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
  - **Marked Neutrophilic Leukocytosis:** Total WBC $15,000–35,000/\mu\text{L}$ with prominent bandemia ($> 15\%$) and toxic granulation. Severe leukopenia ($< 3,000/\mu\text{L}$) occurs in fulminant septic shock with endotoxemia and portends high mortality.
  - Thrombocytopenia develops secondary to severe sepsis and DIC; normal or elevated platelets occur in subacute localized presentations.
- **Biochemical & Inflammatory Markers:**
  - **Severe Acute Phase Response:** C-reactive protein (CRP) is massively elevated ($> 150–300\text{ mg/L}$); erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) $> 80\text{ mm/h}$; procalcitonin markedly elevated ($> 2–10\text{ ng/mL}$).
  - **Marked Hyperglycemia:** Admission blood glucose is frequently $> 200–400\text{ mg/dL}$ with elevated HbA1c ($> 9–12\%$), unmasking latent diabetes mellitus.
  - Elevated serum creatinine and BUN reflecting sepsis-induced acute tubular necrosis.
  - Liver function demonstrates elevated alkaline phosphatase and mild-to-moderate transaminitis.
- **Bedside Imaging (Ultrasound & CT at Triage):**
  - **Abdominal Ultrasound / CT:** Multiple round, well-demarcated hypoechoic microabscesses in the spleen and liver giving the classic **"Swiss cheese / honeycomb" pattern**.
  - **Pelvic / Transrectal Ultrasound:** Hypoechoic fluid collections in the prostate gland in febrile males with urinary symptoms.
  - **Chest Radiography:** Upper lobe alveolar consolidation, rapid cavitation (thin- or thick-walled cavitary lesions), or diffuse nodular bilateral infiltrates in bacteremic dissemination. Absence of mediastinal lymphadenopathy (unlike primary tuberculosis).

## 4. DIFFERENTIAL RULE-OUT CRITERIA (Look-Alikes)
- **Pulmonary Tuberculosis (*M. tuberculosis*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Upper lobe cavitary infiltrates, chronic cough, night sweats, hemoptysis.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* TB exhibits a chronic insidious course (weeks to months), normal to mildly elevated WBC count, and absence of acute prostatic or splenic microabscesses. Acute melioidosis evolves over days with high fever, acute marked leukocytosis (WBC $> 15,000/\mu\text{L}$), and high procalcitonin.
- **Community-Acquired Bacterial Pneumonia (*S. pneumoniae*, *K. pneumoniae*):**
  - *Differentiator:* Acute lobar consolidation, cough, fever.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Standard community pneumonia responds rapidly to ampicillin, ceftriaxone, or respiratory quinolones within 48–72 hours. *B. pseudomallei* is intrinsically resistant to ceftriaxone and does not respond.
- **Scrub Typhus / Leptospirosis:**
  - *Differentiator:* Acute tropical fever after rural vegetation or floodwater contact.
  - *Exclusion Rule:* Scrub typhus presents with an eschar, sensory deafness, and responds to doxycycline; leptospirosis presents with conjunctival suffusion, severe calf tenderness, and direct jaundice. Neither produces splenic "honeycomb" abscesses or prostatic abscesses.
- **Disease-Specific Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Complete absence of travel or residence in a tropical endemic zone (Southeast Asia, Northern Australia, South Asia, Latin America) virtually excludes melioidosis.
  - A healthy, non-diabetic host with no outdoor soil/water exposure presenting with vague fever and normal inflammatory markers (CRP $< 10\text{ mg/L}$) should NOT be attributed to Melioidosis.

## 5. EMPIRICAL DRUG RESPONSE CLUES
- **Critical Intrinsic Antibiotic Resistance Profile:**
  - *Burkholderia pseudomallei* possesses extensive, intrinsic multidrug resistance mechanisms:
  - **INTRINSICALLY RESISTANT TO:** Penicillins, Ampicillin, 1st- and 2nd-generation cephalosporins, **Ceftriaxone / Cefotaxime (3rd-gen cephalosporins are completely ineffective)**, Macrolides (azithromycin, clarithromycin), Aminoglycosides (gentamicin, tobramycin, amikacin), Colistin, and Rifamycins.
  - **WARNING:** Standard empirical sepsis or pneumonia protocols using Ceftriaxone, Vancomycin, or Ciprofloxacin will FAIL, resulting in clinical deterioration.
- **Definitive Treatment Protocol:**
  - **1. Acute Intensive Phase (Minimum 10–14 days, up to 4–8 weeks for deep abscesses or CNS disease):**
    - Intravenous **Ceftazidime** (2 g IV every 6–8 hours, or 50 mg/kg up to 2 g Q8H) OR intravenous **Meropenem** (1 g IV every 8 hours; 2 g IV every 8 hours in septic shock or neurological disease).
    - Median time to fever clearance is prolonged: **9 to 10 days** on appropriate ceftazidime therapy. Continued fever during the first week does NOT signify antibiotic failure.
  - **2. Eradication Phase (Minimum 3 to 6 months):**
    - Oral **Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)** (320/1600 mg [two double-strength tablets] orally twice daily, adjusted for renal function) with oral folic acid (5 mg daily). Crucial to eliminate intracellular persisters and prevent fatal late relapse.
